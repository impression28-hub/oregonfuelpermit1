What is a commercial vehicle trip permit?

A trip permit is a temporary, single-use authorization that lets a commercial motor vehicle travel into, out of, or through Oregon on one specific haul, without holding permanent Oregon apportioned registration (IRP) or an active Oregon weight-mile tax account. It's the standard option for one-off or infrequent runs — a load headed to Portland from out of state, a truck passing through on I-5 or I-84, or a single delivery that doesn't justify setting up ongoing Oregon credentials.

Oregon is one of the few states that taxes commercial trucking by the mile rather than through fuel tax alone. Carriers who already run under IFTA in other states but don't have a standing Oregon weight-mile tax account still need a way to cover the specific miles they'll drive inside Oregon — that's exactly what a single-trip permit is for. It's a lightweight alternative to registering a truck permanently in a state you may only pass through a few times a year.

Who typically needs one

In general, this applies to any commercial vehicle over 26,000 lbs gross weight that will enter, exit, or cross Oregon without a standing Oregon weight-mile tax account or apportioned registration that already covers Oregon travel. Common situations include:

  • An interstate carrier based outside Oregon making a single delivery in-state
  • A truck passing through Oregon en route to another state on I-5, I-84, or U.S. 97
  • A leased or owner-operated vehicle without an active Oregon account
  • A Canadian-plated carrier hauling freight through the Pacific Northwest
  • A dispatcher who needs a same-day permit for an unplanned Oregon leg
